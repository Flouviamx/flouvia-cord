## What a workflow is

A workflow is a rule that watches your Cord account. When a specific event happens (a client opens a quote, a deposit arrives, an invoice becomes overdue, a dispute is opened), the workflow runs the steps you defined, in order: create a task, email your team, post to Slack or add a note in HubSpot.

The point is that follow-up does not depend on someone remembering. If your team checks by hand which quotes nobody opened, who paid the deposit or which invoices are overdue, a workflow does it for you and leaves the task ready for the right person.

## Where to find it

Open **Workflows** in the app sidebar. The list shows each workflow as a card with its name, the event that starts it, its status (**Draft**, **Active** or **Paused**), how many times it ran in the last 30 days and how many of those runs failed. At the top you see how many active workflows you have against your plan limit.

<Callout type="info">
Cord Workflows is for admins. You need the **Settings** permission to view, create, edit or publish workflows. People without it see "Cord Workflows is for admins" and can ask the account owner for access.
</Callout>

## The pieces of a workflow

Each workflow reads top to bottom in the editor, in two blocks: **When this happens** and **Do this**.

| Piece | What it does | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | What starts the workflow: one of Cord's 44 events, or a **fixed schedule** ("every Monday at 9"). Each workflow has exactly one. | [Triggers](/en/docs/automatizacion/workflows/disparadores) |
| **Action** | Something Cord does for you: create a task, email the team, email the client, expire a quote, approve an internal request, void an invoice, send the data to a URL, post to Slack or add a note in HubSpot. | [Actions](/en/docs/automatizacion/workflows/acciones) |
| **Condition** | Splits the flow into two branches, **If the condition is met** and **If the condition isn't met**, based on the event data. | [Conditions](/en/docs/automatizacion/workflows/condiciones) |
| **Wait** | Pauses the flow for 1 to 30 days and then continues. It can also wait **until something happens**, with a time limit. | [Waits](/en/docs/automatizacion/workflows/esperas) |
| **Lookup** | Brings a number from your account — overdue receivables, pipeline, client balance — to use in the steps that follow. | [Lookups](/en/docs/automatizacion/workflows/consultas) |
| **Event data** | Values like `{{cliente}}`, `{{folio}}` or `{{total}}` that you can put in your texts and that are filled in when the workflow runs. | [Event data](/en/docs/automatizacion/workflows/datos-del-evento) |

## A complete example

"Quote follow-up ladder" is one of the ideas Cord ships with. It reads like this:

1. **When this happens:** a quote is sent.
2. **Wait until** the client opens or approves it, with a 2-day limit.
3. **If it happens:** creates the task "Call `{{cliente}}` while `{{folio}}` is warm".
4. **If the time runs out:** emails the client asking if anything needs clearing up, and waits another 5 days for a decision.
5. **If they still do not decide:** looks up the client's balance and tells the owner what they already owe.

Every quote you send starts its own run of this workflow, and each one checks its own quote: the one the client opened within the hour goes one way, the one nobody touched goes the other.

## Draft, published version and runs

- **Draft:** what you edit. Change it as often as you like without affecting anything.
- **Published version:** what runs. It only changes when you click **Publish** or **Publish changes**.
- **Run:** every time the event happens, Cord creates a run with a copy of the version published at that moment. If you publish changes later, runs already in progress finish with the version they started with.

All of this is explained in [Publish, pause and delete](/en/docs/automatizacion/workflows/publicar) and [Runs and errors](/en/docs/automatizacion/workflows/ejecuciones).

## What a workflow never does

<Callout type="warning">
A workflow **never charges, never issues invoices or CFDIs, never records payments, and never approves or rejects a quote on your client's behalf.** Those decisions stay with a person or with the client.

What it does do to your documents is narrow and explicit: email the document's client (never an address typed into the step), expire a quote that is past its validity date, approve an internal approval request, and void an invoice that has no payments yet. Each of those actions only applies when the trigger carries the document it acts on; otherwise the workflow cannot be published.
</Callout>

It also cannot create infinite loops: a workflow is never triggered by the events it causes itself, and a chain of workflows triggering each other stops after three levels. Details in [Limits](/en/docs/automatizacion/workflows/limites).
