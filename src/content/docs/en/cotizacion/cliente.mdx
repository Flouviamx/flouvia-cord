## Ways to add clients

When starting a new quote, the first step of the editor is **Who are you quoting?**. Cord offers different ways to nourish your database:

1. **Manual creation:** directly from the quoter, open the client selector and choose **+ Create new client…** to fill out a quick form with their commercial and fiscal data. You can also create them from **Clients** with **+ New client**.
2. **Bulk import (CSV):** if you are migrating from another CRM or ERP, go to **Clients** in the main menu, use **Import CSV**, download the example template, and upload your file. Each import accepts up to **2,000 rows**; it loads company, contact, email, phone, tax ID, payment terms, and credit limit (the price tier and CFDI tax details are completed later on the client's profile).
3. **API synchronization:** for development teams, Cord offers a public REST API to create and sync clients automatically from your own internal system.

## Price tiers and automatic discount

In business-to-business sales, not all clients pay the same. When creating or editing a client, under **Price tier** you can assign one of four tiers. Each one suggests a discount that you can freely change in the **Automatic discount on their prices** field:

- **Standard:** no suggested discount.
- **Silver:** 5% suggested.
- **Gold:** 10% suggested.
- **Distributor:** 15% suggested.

**How does it work?** When you select that client in your quote, Cord automatically lowers the **Negotiated** price of each product from your catalog by the configured percentage (for example, with 10%, a product listed at $1,000 comes in at $900). Your catalog's list price doesn't change, and you can adjust the negotiated price of any line by hand.

## Commercial and Fiscal Information

For a client profile to be complete, it consists of two parts:

### Commercial information
- **Company** (required): the client's name (e.g., *Flouvia*). It's also the name used as the recipient on their invoice, so write it as it appears on their tax documents if you'll invoice their tax ID.
- **Contact:** the person making the decision (e.g., *André Valle*).
- **Email:** where the quote link is sent when you send it from Cord.
- **Phone**, **Credit limit**, and **Payment terms** (Cash, Net 30, or Net 60) proposed by default when you quote them.

### Fiscal information
- **Tax ID:** the label adapts to the client's country — **RFC** in Mexico, and the
  local equivalent everywhere else (NIF/CIF in Spain, EIN/Tax ID in the US, etc.). Cord
  stores it in uppercase with leading and trailing spaces removed; it doesn't validate
  its structure, so double-check it's typed correctly.
- **Address:** country, postal code, address, city, and state/province, in the collapsible **Address** section.

If the client is Mexican (or you don't assign a country and your business is in Mexico), the form also shows the **Tax details for CFDI (Mexico)** section, with two fields exclusive to CFDI 4.0:
- **Tax regime:** the regime under which they file with the SAT (e.g., *601 - General de Ley Personas Morales*).
- **CFDI use:** the reason they're requesting the invoice (e.g., *G03 - Gastos en general*, which is used if you leave it unspecified).

Leaving these fields for later still leaves your quote commercially valid. In Mexico,
if the client has no RFC on file, Cord stamps the CFDI to **"general public"** (público
en general). If they have an RFC but you leave the tax regime empty, Cord uses regime
601; check that the RFC, postal code, and regime match their tax status certificate
(constancia de situación fiscal) before stamping.

## The client profile

From **Clients** in the main menu, your directory has quick filters (with quotes,
not quoted yet, with a tier, with credit, missing fiscal data) and a search box — handy
once your directory grows into hundreds of contacts.

Click any client to open their **profile**: it's not just the edit form anymore, it's a
full picture of what Cord already knows about that relationship — how much you've
closed with them, how much sits in open pipeline, their real close rate, how much of
their credit limit is in use (with a visual warning if they're over it), the discount
you've given them in negotiation, their activity over the last 12 months, their recent
quotes, and which products they buy most. Editing and deleting are still one click away
from there.

<Callout type="info">
**Tip:** if a client has a credit limit configured, their profile shows you right away
how much open balance they carry against that limit — the same signal the risk engine
uses to block (or allow) their next quote on credit.
</Callout>
