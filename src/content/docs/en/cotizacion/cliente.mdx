## What it is

The **Who are you quoting?** step of the editor links the quote to a client in your
directory. Three things come from their profile: their price tier discount, their
payment terms, and the recipient data the invoice will use.

## Requirements

- **Permission:** Quotes to pick a client; **Clients** to create a new one.
- **Plan:** Free allows up to 50 clients and Starter up to 500; from Pro there's no cap.
- **To send:** the quote needs a client. A draft can be saved without one, but on send Cord asks you to "Choose a client in step 1 to be able to send".

## Pick or create the client

<Steps>
1. In **Who are you quoting?**, open the selector and pick a client from your directory.
2. If they don't exist, choose **+ Create new client…**, fill in the form, and click **Create and select**. They're saved in your directory and selected on the quote.
3. Check the preselected payment terms and change them if needed.
</Steps>

Other ways to fill your directory:

- **From Clients:** **+ New client** for one, or **Import CSV** to load up to 2,000 rows per file (company, contact, email, phone, tax ID, payment terms, and credit limit).
- **From a client's profile:** **New quote** opens the editor with that client already selected.
- **Through the API:** the [clients API](/en/docs/desarrolladores/funciones/clientes) creates and syncs clients from your system.

## Price tier and automatic discount

Each client can have a **Price tier** with a suggested discount you can change in the
automatic discount field:

| Tier | Suggested discount |
|---|---|
| Standard | 0% |
| Silver | 5% |
| Gold | 10% |
| Distributor | 15% |

When you pick the client, Cord lowers the **Negotiated** price of each catalog product
by that percentage: with 10%, a product listed at 1,000 comes in at 900. The list price
doesn't change and you can adjust any line's negotiated price by hand.

## Commercial and tax information

### Commercial

- **Company** (required): the client's name. It's also the recipient name on their invoice; write it as it appears on their tax documents.
- **Contact:** the person who decides.
- **Email:** where the link is sent when you send the quote from Cord.
- **Phone**, **Credit limit**, and **Payment terms** (Cash, Net 30, or Net 60) proposed when you quote them.

### Tax

- **Tax ID:** the label adapts to the client's country: **RFC** in Mexico, NIF/CIF in Spain, EIN/Tax ID in the United States, and so on. Cord stores it uppercased and trimmed; it doesn't validate its structure.
- **Address:** country, postal code, address, city, and state or province, in the **Address** section.

If the client is Mexican (or you don't set a country and your business is in Mexico),
a **CFDI tax data (Mexico)** section appears with two CFDI 4.0 fields:

- **Tax regime**, for example *601 - General de Ley Personas Morales*.
- **CFDI use**, for example *G03 - Gastos en general*, which is used if you leave it empty.

## Behavior and edge cases

- **Incomplete tax data:** the quote is still commercially valid. In Mexico, without an RFC the CFDI is issued to "público en general" (the general public); with an RFC but no regime, Cord uses 601. Check RFC, postal code, and regime against the client's tax certificate before stamping.
- **Changing the client after sending** isn't possible with **Modify and resend**: duplicate the quote and send it to the right client.
- **Editing the profile** doesn't rewrite fiscal documents already issued: they keep the recipient data they were issued with.
- **Credit limit:** if the client has one, their profile shows how much open balance they have against it. It's a signal for you, not a block.

## Common problems

**"+ Create new client…" is missing or fails to save.** Your role doesn't have the Clients permission, or you reached your plan's client cap.

**The tier discount wasn't applied.** It only lowers catalog products; free lines have no list price.

**The CFDI was issued to "público en general".** The client had no RFC when you stamped.
