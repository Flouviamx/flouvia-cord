## The "OK" via email problem

Traditionally, a sales rep sends a PDF and the client replies "Authorized, proceed." It can later be difficult to show which version, line items, or amount each person was reviewing.

Cord adds an **electronic approval and technical evidence** layer at that moment.

## The approval process

When your client clicks **Approve quote** inside the link, the same quote card moves to the **Digital signature** step (it's not a separate window), with a summary of the folio and the total being approved.

To proceed, the client must:
1. **Type their full name** in *Signer's full name* (at least two characters).
2. **Check the box** *"I have reviewed this quote and accept the stated terms and conditions"*.

The *Email (optional)* field can be left blank. The **Confirm and sign** button is enabled as soon as there's a name and the box is checked.

If you changed the proposal while the client was reviewing it, Cord doesn't accept a signature on the previous version: it asks them to review the changes before approving.

### Partial approval

Before approving, the client can uncheck the line items they don't want to include (each line has its own *Include this line in your approval* checkbox). The signature covers only the included lines: Cord recalculates subtotal, taxes, and total with those lines, and the rest are marked as **Not included**.

### Rejection with a reason

If the client disagrees, they can use **Reject**, write a *Reason (optional)*, and **Confirm rejection**. The reason shows up in the quote's activity, and the rejection triggers the matching notification (see [Notifications](/en/docs/gestion/notificaciones)).

### Audit trail data

At confirmation, Cord stores a **technical audit trail**:
- **Signer's name** and, if they entered it, their email.
- **IP address** from which the approval was made and the browser used.
- **Date and time** of the signature, recorded by Cord's database at the moment it's saved.
- **Cryptographic fingerprint (SHA-256)** computed over the quote, the type of approval (full or partial), the signer's name, the IP, and the approved lines (description, quantity, prices, and tax rate), used to detect later changes to the data covered by the approval.

## The Audit Stamp

Once approved, the quote's link shows an **audit stamp** in the web view, with this content:

> **Document verified** — **Signer:** [Signer's name] (IP: [IP address]) — **Date:** [date and time] — **Seal:** [SHA-256 fingerprint]

The signer's email doesn't appear on the stamp. The quote PDF you download from Cord doesn't include the stamp; in the quote's details, the activity records who signed, from which IP, and, for a partial approval, how many lines and what amount they accepted.

### What it is useful for
- **Preparing a dispute:** if the client disputes a card charge, the record can form part of the evidence you submit. The issuing bank decides the outcome, and Cord does not guarantee that a dispute will be won.
- **Partial approval, also signed:** the fingerprint covers exactly the lines the client accepted, not the original total.
- **Collections context:** the team can review who accepted, when, and for what amount instead of relying only on a verbal "yes" or a stray email.
- **Connecting the commercial workflow:** the approved version can be used to begin collection and prepare the invoice available for the account's market.
