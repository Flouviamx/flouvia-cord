## The "OK" via email problem

Traditionally, a sales rep sends a PDF and the client replies "Authorized, proceed." It can later be difficult to show which version, line items, or amount each person was reviewing.

Cord adds an **electronic approval and technical evidence** layer at that moment.

## The approval process

When your client clicks **Approve Quote** inside the link, Cord shows a confirmation modal before changing the status.

To proceed, the client must:
1. **Enter their Full Name.**
2. **Enter their Corporate Email.**
3. **Check a confirmation box** declaring that they have authority to accept the terms and commercial obligation described in the quote.

### Audit trail data

At confirmation, Cord stores a **technical audit trail**:
- **Public IP Address** from which the approval was made.
- **Exact Timestamp** synchronized with secure servers.
- **Cryptographic fingerprint** of the approved version and scope, used to detect later changes to the data covered by the approval.

## The Audit Stamp

Once approved, the quote's status changes visually. At the bottom of the document (both in the web view and the subsequently generated downloadable PDF), an **Audit Stamp** is permanently embedded.

This stamp clearly shows:
> *"Digitally approved by [Client Name] ([Client Email]) on [Date and Time] from IP address [192.168.x.x]."*

### What it is useful for
- **Preparing a dispute:** if the client disputes a card charge, the record can form part of the evidence you submit. The issuing bank decides the outcome, and Cord does not guarantee that a dispute will be won.
- **Partial approval, also signed:** if your client only wants to accept some line items from a lengthy quote, they can approve it partially — the same audit stamp covers exactly the lines and amount they accepted, not the original total.
- **Collections context:** the team can review who accepted, when, and for what amount instead of relying only on a verbal "yes" or a stray email.
- **Connecting the commercial workflow:** the approved version can be used to begin collection and prepare the invoice available for the account's market.

<Callout type="warning">
Cord stores technical evidence of approval; it does not provide notarial services or legal advice. Validity and evidentiary weight depend on the document, workflow, and jurisdiction. Consult a professional for high-risk cases.
</Callout>
