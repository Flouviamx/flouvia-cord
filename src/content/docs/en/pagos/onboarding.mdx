## What it is

To collect by card or SPEI from a quote or invoice link you need a verified **payments account** in your business's name. Financial regulations require knowing who you are, who controls the business, and where the money goes (KYC, *Know Your Customer*). Cord runs the whole process **inside the app**: the wizard, the camera, and the bank account are Cord screens, with no redirects to a third-party form.

On payments in Mexican pesos (MXN), Cord charges a fee per payment; in any other currency there's no platform fee today. See [Cord Payments fees](/en/docs/pagos/condiciones).

## Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Plan | All plans. |
| Permission | **Payment settings**. |
| Country | Mexico, United States, Canada, Brazil, Spain, United Kingdom, Germany, and France. |
| Documents | A valid, color government ID for each person you declare. |
| Bank account | In the name of the business or its legal representative, in your country's banking format. |

<Callout type="warning" title="Check the country before you start">
The payments account is opened in your business country (**Settings › General**) and in that country's currency, and **neither can be changed later**: regulatory verification requires it. Cord blocks country changes once the account exists. If the country is wrong, fix it before you start.
</Callout>

### Where it's available

Cord Payments works today in **Mexico, the United States, Canada, Brazil, Spain, the United Kingdom, Germany, and France**.

In **Colombia, Argentina, Chile, and Peru** you can use everything else (quote, invoice, follow up on collections, and record payments received by transfer) and collect online with [Mercado Pago](/en/docs/pagos/mercado-pago). **Settings › Payments** tells you Cord Payments isn't available before you start a verification that couldn't be completed.

## The 8 wizard steps

<Steps>
1. Go to **Settings › Payments**.
2. If you haven't connected your account yet, the wizard appears on that same screen, at step 1. Move forward with **Continue**.
3. Complete the steps. Whatever you save is kept: if you leave halfway, you pick up where you left off.
</Steps>

| Step | What it captures |
|---|---|
| **1. Entity type** | Whether you operate as an **Individual** or a **Company**, and your line of business. |
| **2. Business details** | Legal name, your country's tax ID (RFC in Mexico, NIF/CIF in Spain, EIN in the United States), and contact details. |
| **3. Registered address** | The business's registered tax address. |
| **4. Identity** | Personal details of the legal representative or the individual. Only the fields your country's verification requires appear: in the United States, for example, the last 4 digits of the SSN; in Spain, Germany, and the United Kingdom no personal ID number is requested. |
| **5. People** | Who controls the business, with their role and ownership. An individual skips this step. See [People and beneficial owners](#people-and-beneficial-owners). |
| **6. Verification** | The photo of each person's ID and, if requested, a proof of address or a company document. |
| **7. Bank account** | The payout account in your country's format. See [Payouts to your bank](/en/docs/pagos/depositos#your-payout-account). |
| **8. Terms** | You accept the processing of your data, the Cord Payments terms, and your payments account agreement. The acceptance is recorded with date, IP address, and terms version. The Cord fee is activated separately, in **Settings › Payments** (see [Fees](/en/docs/pagos/condiciones#how-your-fee-schedule-is-activated)). |

If verification finds something specific missing (a blurry photo, a detail that doesn't match), the wizard takes you straight to that step instead of starting over.

## Verification decides what's requested

Cord has no fixed list of fields per country. The data, documents, and declarations you see come from what your account's verification reports as pending, and the country only decides **what each item is called** (RFC, NIF, EIN; INE, DNI, Personalausweis). That's why two businesses in the same country can see different steps: a company with several partners declares more people than an individual, and a new requirement appears as soon as verification asks for it.

## People and beneficial owners

In the European Union, the United Kingdom, and Canada, declaring **beneficial owners** (people with 25% or more ownership) is a legal obligation. The **People** step is a list you build:

- **Add each person** with their details, ownership, and roles.
- **A person can have several roles**, each marked separately:

| Role | When to mark it |
|---|---|
| **Legal representative** | Operates the account and signs for the business. |
| **Owner** | Owns 25% or more of the business. Their percentage is captured. |
| **Director** | Is a director, administrator, or board member. |
| **Executive** | Holds an executive management role. Their job title is captured. |

- **The total percentage is informational.** Cord shows "Declared: N% ownership", but doesn't force you to reach 100%: there are legitimate structures where the named owners don't add up to the total.
- **Each person has their own verification**, with a visible status: **Details captured**, **Under review**, **Verified**, or **Action required**.
- **Adding, editing, or removing a person requires confirming your identity**, just like changing the payout account.

A partnership with three partners at 30% each declares all three. If it declared only one, the account wouldn't activate: verification would keep asking for the missing ones.

### Declarations

When your country's verification requires it, a declaration appears at the end of the list, for example: "I confirm I've added every owner with ≥25%". It's a **legal declaration under your responsibility** and is sent as is. It only appears **after** you've built the list, and Cord records on its server the date, IP address, and browser from which you accepted it.

<Callout type="info" title="What Cord does NOT keep">
ID numbers, dates of birth, home addresses, and document images go straight to the financial verification system. Cord keeps each person's name and roles to show you the list. That's why, when you edit a person, the date of birth, ID, and address are entered again; if you leave them blank, the ones already on file are kept.
</Callout>

## Identity documents by country

Before taking the photo, you choose which document you'll use. That choice adjusts the shape of the camera frame (a card or a passport page) and decides whether you'll be asked for the back.

| Country | Suggested documents | Back side |
|---|---|---|
| Mexico | Voter ID (INE), passport, driving licence | INE and licence yes; passport no |
| United States | State driving licence, state ID card, passport | Licence and ID yes; passport no |
| Canada | Passport, provincial driving licence, provincial ID card | Licence and ID yes; passport no |
| Brazil | CNH, RG, passport | CNH and RG yes; passport no |
| Spain | DNI, NIE / TIE, passport, driving licence | DNI, NIE / TIE, and licence yes; passport no |
| United Kingdom | Passport, photocard driving licence, biometric residence permit | Licence and permit yes; passport no |
| Germany | *Personalausweis*, passport, electronic residence permit | Yes, except the passport |
| France | *Carte nationale d'identité*, passport, *titre de séjour* | Yes, except the passport |

This list translates the three categories verification accepts (passport, driving licence, and government-issued photo ID) into the name you recognize your document by. It's not a guarantee of acceptance: if verification rejects a document, Cord tells you why.

<Callout type="warning" title="If you live in another country, passport only">
If a person's country of residence differs from the payments account's country, verification **only accepts a passport**. Cord detects this and tells you before you take the photo, so you don't wait days for a certain rejection.
</Callout>

### Proof of address and company documents

If verification asks for it, a **proof of address** is added: a recent utility bill or bank statement in the person's name. It must be a **different** document from the ID: one document can't cover two requirements. If a **company document** is requested, it's uploaded in the same step.

| File | Formats | Maximum size |
|---|---|---|
| ID (front and back) | JPG or PNG, in color | 10 MB |
| Proof of address or company document, from the computer | JPG, PNG, or PDF | 10 MB |
| Any photo from the phone | JPG or PNG | 10 MB |

## Continue on your phone

A phone camera almost always gives better light and focus than a webcam or a scan. In the **Verification** step, the **With your phone** option is marked as recommended.

<Steps>
1. On your computer, in the **Verification** step, choose **With your phone** and click **Generate QR code**. You can also copy the link with **Copy** and open it on your phone.
2. Scan the code with your phone's camera. **Verify your identity** opens with the steps you'll need; tap **Start**.
3. Choose which document you'll use. If your address is in another country, the screen tells you only a passport is accepted.
4. Take the photo of the **front**. If you prefer the camera app or already have the photo, use **Choose a photo instead**.
5. Take the photo of the **back**, if your document has one. With a passport, tap **Skip — it's a passport**.
6. If requested, take the photo of the **proof of address**. You can skip it when it wasn't requested.
7. When you finish you'll see **Done**. Go back to your computer: the screen, which says "Waiting for you to finish on your phone…", continues on its own.
</Steps>

### Link rules

The QR code link is a single-use credential and is treated as one:

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Validity | 30 minutes from when you generate it, and 15 minutes from the first photo you upload. Whichever comes first. |
| One device | It's tied to the first phone that uploads a photo. You can move from a messaging app's browser to the same phone's browser, but if it's used on more devices it's blocked with "For security this link is tied to the first phone that opened it". |
| Attempts | It allows up to 12 uploads and is blocked after 6 failures in a row. When exhausted it says the link's attempts ran out. |
| Same photo | The same photo isn't accepted twice: if a document was rejected, take a new photo. The same photo isn't accepted for front and back either. |
| Privacy | The link page records no analytics and isn't stored in the browser cache. |

If the link expires or is blocked, generate a new one from your computer with **Generate a new one**. What you already uploaded is kept.

## Photo quality

The most common cause of a rejection isn't an invalid document: it's a photo that can't be read. Before sending, Cord measures the image. Almost everything it detects is **advice**: you can send the photo anyway if you think it's fine. Only two things stop the upload, because verification always rejects them:

| What Cord detects | What it does | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Very low resolution | **Blocks** | Move closer to the document, or use **Choose a photo** to take it with the camera app. |
| ID in black and white | **Blocks** | Take it again in color. |
| Photo too dark | Warns | Move near a window or turn on a light. |
| Too much light | Warns | Move away from the lamp or direct sunlight. |
| Glare on the document | Warns | Tilt it slightly to remove it. |
| The document blends into the background | Warns | Place it on a differently colored surface. |
| Blurry photo | Warns | Rest it on a flat surface and wait for it to focus. |
| Readable, but could be better | Warns | Move closer and let it focus. |

A black-and-white proof of address is accepted: the color rule applies only to photo IDs.

## If a document is rejected

When verification rejects a photo, Cord shows **the reason** on that person's card: the image can't be read, the document is expired, the name doesn't match the one entered, or the back is missing. Fix that specific issue and take a new photo.

<Callout type="info" title="Your photos aren't stored in Cord">
Images are transmitted encrypted to the financial verification system and Cord doesn't store them. Before sending them, Cord **strips their metadata**, including the GPS location many phones save inside the photo, and keeps only the orientation so the photo doesn't arrive rotated.

Cord does keep a **compliance record** of each upload: which side of the document was sent, when, from which IP address, the photo's quality measurements, and what verification answered. Never the image, the document number, or a thumbnail. That record is kept for five years, the period required by anti-money-laundering rules.
</Callout>

## Account statuses

| Status | What you see in Settings › Payments | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| **New** | The wizard at step 1. | Quote, invoice, and record manual payments. Card and SPEI are locked. |
| **Pending** | **Verification pending**: verification is in progress or a requirement is missing. The screen refreshes on its own during review. | Complete what's pending. Card and SPEI stay locked. |
| **Active** | **All set, you can accept payments now!**: identity and bank account confirmed. | Collect with the methods you turn on and [receive payouts](/en/docs/pagos/depositos). |

An active account can ask for information again later, for example when a document expires or requirements change. The **Account status** widget on [Revenue › Payments](/en/docs/pagos/dashboard) shows how many requirements are missing and their deadline.

## Limits

- Cord Payments is available in eight countries; the account's country and currency don't change later.
- Cord doesn't run liveness checks or selfies: document authenticity is checked by the verification system on the photo you upload.
- The phone link lasts at most 30 minutes and allows 12 uploads.
- IDs are uploaded as JPG or PNG, in color, up to 10 MB.

## Common problems

**The wizard asks for something another account didn't.** What's requested depends on your country, entity type, and verification status. That's normal.

**The camera won't start on my phone.** Check that the browser has camera permission. If you opened the link inside a messaging app, open it in the phone's browser. You can always use **Choose a photo instead**.

**It says the link is tied to another phone.** Generate a new link from your computer and open it only on the phone you'll use.

**The same photo was rejected again.** Resending the same file fails again. Take a new photo fixing the reason shown.

**I can't change the business country.** That's intentional once the payments account exists: the account's country can't be modified.
