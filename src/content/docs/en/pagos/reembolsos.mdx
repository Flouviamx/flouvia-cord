## Where to refund

It depends on how the money came in:

| The payment came in through… | Refund it from… |
|---|---|
| A **quote** link (full payment, deposit, balance or installments) or a [retainer](/en/docs/pagos/igualas) | **Revenue › Payments** (`/app/cobros`), with the **Refund** button in "Recent payments". |
| An **invoice** link, the [client portal](/en/docs/pagos/portal-facturas) or [automatic payments](/en/docs/pagos/cobro-automatico) | The **invoice detail page**, with the **Refund** button next to the payment. See [Refunding an invoice payment](#refunding-an-invoice-payment). |
| A quote link, collected with **Mercado Pago** | Your Mercado Pago account; Cord reads it and records it on its own (see [Mercado Pago](/en/docs/pagos/mercado-pago)). |
| A **manually recorded** payment (transfer, cash, check) | Outside Cord: Cord did not move that money, so there is no button. You handle the refund directly with your customer. |

In **Revenue › Payments**, every row in "Recent payments" that corresponds to a payment **reconciled through Cord Payments** has the **Refund** button. Once a refund is confirmed, Cord reflects it in the balance of each invoice that money paid (see [Effect on a linked invoice](#effect-on-a-linked-invoice)).

<Callout type="warning">
Refunding requires the **Refunds** permission. This permission is **not granted automatically** to team administrators — the account owner has to explicitly grant it to each member who needs to issue refunds, from Settings › Team. This is intentional: moving money out of the account is a more sensitive action than administering it.
</Callout>

## How it works

In Revenue › Payments (an invoice's detail page works the same way, with what is explained [below](#refunding-an-invoice-payment)):

1. Click **Refund** on the payment's row.
2. A dialog opens with the amount available to refund (it may be less than the total, if you already issued a partial refund before) and an optional internal reason field.
3. Enter the amount — the full total or just a portion.
4. Tick the box confirming you understand that the refund returns the stated amount to the customer but **does not automatically refund Cord's platform fee**. You can't confirm without it.
5. Since this moves money, Cord asks you to **re-confirm your identity** (your password or your 2FA code) before executing the refund, even if your session was already active. It's the same "re-authentication" mechanism that protects other sensitive account actions.
6. The refund is logged in your organization's audit trail, with the amount, the reason (if given), and who authorized it.

You can refund the same payment **more than once**, as long as the accumulated total doesn't exceed what was originally charged — the dialog always shows you how much is still available.

## Refunding an invoice payment

<Callout type="info">
This feature is new and may not be enabled on your account yet. If you don't see it, write to us.
</Callout>

On an invoice's detail page, the **Payments** section lists each payment. An online payment —by card, SEPA debit, ACH debit or Mercado Pago— that came in through the invoice link, the client portal or automatic payments has the **Refund** button. Under each payment you see what has already been refunded and what is in progress.

1. Click **Refund** next to the payment.
2. Cord checks the payment and tells you what can be returned **before** you confirm: the available amount, whether it accepts a partial amount and, if it can't be refunded, why.
3. Enter the amount (or confirm the total), add an internal reason if you want, and check the box confirming that Cord's platform fee is not refunded automatically.
4. Confirm. Cord asks you to confirm your identity again if you haven't in the last 10 minutes, just like in Payments.
5. The refund is recorded in the audit log and in the invoice's activity.

### What each method allows

| Method | Refund | Window and timing |
|---|---|---|
| Card | Full or partial | The money goes back to the client's card within their bank's timeframes. |
| SEPA debit (EUR) | Full or partial | Up to 180 days after the charge. It takes 3 to 4 business days to process and up to 5 to reach the client's account. |
| ACH debit (USD) | **Full only** | Up to 180 days after the charge. It takes up to 3 business days and reaches the client as a credit that doesn't say "refund": let them know. |
| Mercado Pago | Full or partial | It leaves your connected Mercado Pago account and goes back to the method the client paid with. |

- A **bank debit in progress** doesn't appear among the invoice's payments yet, and it can't be refunded until it's confirmed. The invoice detail page says so.
- A **SPEI bank transfer** payment is not refunded from the invoice: return it by transfer from your bank.
- A payment that **came in through the quote** (the quote was already paid, fully or partially, when it was invoiced) is refunded from its payment in Revenue › Payments, or from your Mercado Pago account if Mercado Pago collected it. The invoice reflects it on its own.
- A **manually recorded** payment has no button: Cord did not move that money and can't return it.
- If the provider has a refund of that payment that Cord hasn't recorded yet (for example, one issued outside Cord a few minutes ago), Cord waits to record it before letting you request another.

### What happens on the invoice

- A **confirmed** refund reopens that amount: a paid invoice goes back to open with that balance, and one with an outstanding balance increases it. One **in progress** (bank debits, Mercado Pago under review) doesn't touch the balance until it's confirmed.
- On a **voided** invoice that received a late payment, refunding it lowers the "Amount to return to client".
- The invoice's activity records each refund once: "Refund of …" when it's confirmed, "Refund of … requested" while it's in progress, and "The refund of … did not go through" if it fails. The provider's notice that arrives later doesn't count it twice.
- With Cord Payments, the refund also triggers the `refund.succeeded` and `refund.failed` events of your [webhooks](/en/docs/desarrolladores/herramientas/webhooks), like any other refund.
- The refund **doesn't change the document**: the invoiced amount stays the same. If what you return will no longer be charged, also issue a credit note (see [Effect on a linked invoice](#effect-on-a-linked-invoice)).

### Mexico: the CFDI remains valid

Refunding the payment doesn't cancel or change the stamped CFDI: before the SAT, the invoice remains valid for its original amount, and if that payment had a payment complement, the complement isn't canceled either. To document the return, issue a **credit note** from **More actions › Credit note**: in Mexico it is a CFDI de egreso (type E) with use G02 "Returns, discounts or rebates" and relationship 01 to the invoice's UUID.

Cord doesn't issue it on its own, because not every refund is a return of the sale: refunding a duplicate charge, for example, doesn't change what was invoiced. Decide the amount with your accountant. While there is refunded money without a credit note covering it, the invoice detail page reminds you.

## Card vs. SPEI: two different flows

### Card

The refund is processed **automatically** as soon as you confirm it — no manual step required on your part. The money returns to the customer's original card on whatever timeline their bank handles.

### SPEI

In Cord, the SPEI refund flow requires a manual transfer by the business. Requesting it in the dashboard does not itself return the money:

1. Gets recorded in Cord as "pending, manual refund".
2. Cord automatically creates a task on your to-do list reminding you to transfer the amount back to the customer.
3. You make the transfer yourself through your normal bank account, and mark the task as resolved.

## What happens to your money

- The refunded amount is deducted from your net revenue across all of the [Payments](/en/docs/pagos/dashboard) reports — it never stays "hidden" in the total collected figure.
- **The platform fee Cord charged on the original payment is not refunded automatically**, and the payment processor's fees are governed by its own terms. It's a processing cost that already occurred when the card was charged or the SPEI transfer was received — refunding the customer doesn't reverse that cost. See the details in [Fees and advantages](/en/docs/pagos/condiciones).
- If the refunded payment was part of a quote with an [upfront deposit and balance](/en/docs/pagos/anticipos), the refund only affects the specific payment you chose — not the rest of that quote's slices.

<Callout type="info">
A refund **does not revert the quote's status** — it stays marked as paid. If you also need to cancel the quote itself (for example, because the entire order was called off), do that from the quote's detail page separately.
</Callout>

## Effect on a linked invoice

Cord distinguishes a requested refund from a confirmed one. Only confirmed refunds
count toward money returned on the invoice; a repeated event doesn't subtract it again.
A refund can arrive before the payment record and await that link.

A confirmed refund **reopens** that amount in the invoice balance: the invoice is no
longer paid for that amount. If the refund is for something that won't be collected
anymore (a returned product, a service that wasn't delivered), also issue a **credit
note** for that amount: a credit note reduces the document amount while a refund
returns money, and they're separate actions. Review [balance
calculation](/en/docs/pagos/facturas-emitidas).

## Refunding a charge that paid several invoices

<Callout type="info">
This feature is new and may not be enabled on your account yet. If you don't see it, write to us.
</Callout>

From the [client portal](/en/docs/pagos/portal-facturas) and with [automatic
payments](/en/docs/pagos/cobro-automatico), a single charge can pay several invoices.

**If you refund from Cord**, you do it from the detail page of one of those invoices and choose:

- **This invoice only:** up to what the charge applied to that invoice, minus what has already been returned to it. The refund goes to that invoice, not to the last one.
- **The whole payment:** returns everything left of the charge and reopens each invoice for what it paid.

An **ACH** charge only allows **The whole payment**: returning a single invoice's share would be a partial refund.

**If the refund is issued outside Cord** (or you choose The whole payment), Cord allocates the refund among the invoices the charge paid:

1. **From the last one to the first.** The refund is assigned starting with the last
   invoice the charge was applied to, and works backwards.
2. **Up to what each invoice received.** Each invoice is assigned at most what it
   received from that charge, minus what other refunds of the same charge already
   assigned to it.
3. **All or nothing.** If the refund doesn't fit entirely in what the charge applied
   (for example, because the refund notice arrived before the payment notice), it isn't
   split halfway: it waits and is allocated as soon as the payment is recorded.

| Example | F-0231 | F-0236 | Charge total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Payment applied (in this order) | 48,940.00 MXN | 52,316.40 MXN | 101,256.40 MXN |
| Refund of 60,000.00 MXN | −7,683.60 MXN | −52,316.40 MXN | −60,000.00 MXN |
| Balance that reopens | 7,683.60 MXN | 52,316.40 MXN | |

- A charge that paid **a single** invoice assigns the whole refund to that invoice.
- Refunds of charges from before October 2026 have no allocation: they only count in the
  balance if their charge paid a single invoice.
- An **ACH** bank debit only supports **full** refunds.
