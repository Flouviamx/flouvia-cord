## One document, three regulatory rails

Cord doesn't treat an invoice as a generic PDF with a number: the type of receipt it issues depends on your business's country. Today there are three rails:

- **Mexico:** CFDI 4.0, stamped with the SAT.
- **Spain:** a legally complete invoice plus the certified filing record required by **Verifactu** (RD 1007/2023).
- **Every other country Cord supports:** a complete commercial invoice — with a sequential number, per-line taxes, and full standing between the parties — with no stamping before any authority, because those countries neither require nor offer that mechanism.

In all three cases, tax is calculated **per line**, not per document: you can mix a taxed item with an exempt one on the same invoice, and each one prints the rate and the name of the tax that applies in your country (VAT in the UK, GST in Canada, state-by-state sales tax in the US, IVA in Mexico and Spain, and so on, based on your account's fiscal profile).

## Mexico: Nominative CFDI 4.0

For accounts in Mexico, Cord automatically stamps with the SAT through a certified electronic-invoicing provider — your team never has to log into a separate portal or manage certificates on its own.

### Pulling data from the catalog

Unlike systems that issue to the "General Public" and then force the client to self-invoice through an external portal, Cord is proactive and pulls fiscal data directly from the client's record:

- **Tax Regime:** the one assigned to that client's profile.
- **CFDI Use:** based on the type of product or service, or explicitly set on the client's profile.
- **Zip code:** the one registered on their tax status certificate.

### Automatic, transparent stamping

1. As soon as the payment is confirmed (by card or SPEI through Cord Payments, or recorded manually), Cord assembles the receipt.
2. The line items from the quote or invoice are combined with the client's exact fiscal data (Regime, zip code, CFDI Use).
3. The document is stamped with the SAT as a nominative CFDI 4.0.
4. The XML and PDF are emailed to the client along with their payment receipt, with no manual work from your team.

## Spain: Verifactu

For accounts in Spain, every invoice generates a filing record with a **chained SHA-256 fingerprint**, linked to your organization's previous record — the traceability mechanism required by Royal Decree 1007/2023 (Verifactu). The chain is built at the moment of issuance, together with the rest of the legal data Spanish rules require: a validated NIF/NIE/CIF, IRPF withholding where it applies, series-and-year numbering, and the legal note for reverse-charge intra-EU transactions.

<Callout type="info">
**Online submission to the Spanish tax authority** rolls out in phases as Cord completes the regulatory process with the authority itself. While that submission isn't yet active for your account, the invoice remains a complete, legally valid document between you and your client — the only thing that changes is whether the record has already reached the tax authority or not. Cord always states this clearly on the document, never as an assumption.
</Callout>

## Everywhere else: commercial invoice

In the United States, Canada, Brazil, the United Kingdom, Germany, France, Colombia, Argentina, Chile, and Peru, Cord issues a **complete commercial invoice**: a sequential number, per-line taxes with the name and rate that apply in that country, withholdings where the country has them, and full standing as a receipt between the parties. What doesn't exist in these countries is an automated stamping or filing step with a tax authority — because that mechanism (CFDI, Verifactu) is specific to Mexico and Spain respectively; the rest of the countries neither require nor offer that rail, so the commercial invoice already IS the final document.

## The receipt for Cord Payments' own fee (Mexico)

In addition to the CFDI you stamp for your own customers, in Mexico Cord issues its **own income CFDI** for the fee it charges on every Cord Payments transaction (see [Fees and advantages](/en/docs/pagos/condiciones)) — this is the tax receipt for the payment-processing service Cord provides you, not for your own sale.

This receipt is issued **consolidated, in periodic batches** (not one per individual transaction) against the RFC and fiscal data registered on your account.

<Callout type="warning">
If your RFC, tax regime, or zip code aren't up to date on your profile, Cord's commission CFDI cannot be issued. Keep that information current in **Settings › Fiscal**.
</Callout>
