  <p class="page-subtitle">Your client approves today and pays on the agreed date. Cord separates approval from payment and opens payment when the due date arrives.</p>

## What they are

A **credit term** is the time you give your client to pay after approving. In Cord it's
expressed as **Net N**: N calendar days counted from approval. With **Due on receipt**,
payment is available as soon as the client approves.

When a quote carries a credit term, the public link **doesn't offer to pay upfront**: the
client approves, sees the date their payment is due, and the pay button only appears when
that date arrives.

## Available terms

| Term | Days | Where it appears when quoting |
|---|---:|---|
| **Due on receipt** | 0 | Quick option |
| **Net 15** | 15 | Quick option |
| **Net 30** | 30 | Quick option |
| **Net 60** | 60 | Quick option |
| **Net 7** | 7 | Under **Other term** |
| **Net 45** | 45 | Under **Other term** |
| **Net 90** | 90 | Under **Other term** |

They're the same seven terms in the app, the [API](/en/docs/desarrolladores/funciones/cotizaciones)
(`contado`, `net7`, `net15`, `net30`, `net45`, `net60`, `net90`), the MCP server, and the
integrations. In a Spanish-language account, Due on receipt is called **Contado**.

## Where the term is set

Cord picks the term for a new quote in this order:

1. **The one you choose when quoting**, in **Who are you quoting?**. It always wins.
2. **The one on the client's profile** (the **Terms** field), which Cord preselects when you
   pick them. See [Clients](/en/docs/gestion/clientes).
3. **Your account's**, in **Settings › Quotes › Numbering & tax**, field **Default payment
   terms**.

If the client has no term of their own and you change nothing, the quote starts with your
account's default term.

## How the due date is calculated

The due date is the **approval date plus the term's days**. If the quote isn't approved yet,
Cord counts from its creation date. The same calculation is used by the public link,
[receivables and collections](/en/docs/pagos/cartera-cobranza), the
[reminders](/en/docs/pagos/recordatorios), so they all see the same day.

Example: your client approves with **Net 30** on October 12, 2026; payment is due on
November 11, 2026.

## What your client sees

1. You send the quote with, for example, **Net 30**.
2. Your client opens the link and sees the **Approve quote** button, the same as with Due on
   receipt.
3. On approval, the link shows **Quote approved** and, instead of the payment form, the notice
   **Order confirmed with Net 30 credit** with the line "The payment of [total] is due on
   [date]".
4. Below it, Cord tells them how they'll pay:
   - if you collect online, "Once the date arrives you'll be able to pay online from this same link";
   - if not, "Coordinate payment directly with the seller".

The quote becomes **Approved** and you can deliver the product or service.

### When the due date arrives

On the due date, the same link stops showing the credit notice and shows the pay button
with the methods you have active (see [Payment methods](/en/docs/pagos/metodos)). Before that
date, the quote's payment page sends the client back to the link, which explains when it's due.

### With a deposit

If the quote asks for a [deposit](/en/docs/pagos/anticipos), your client pays it on approval
and the **balance** is due on the term's date. The link shows the payment plan with the
balance marked "Due on [date]" until the day comes.

### Invoices

An **invoice** can be paid as soon as you issue it: its due date marks when reminders and
collections start, not when payment opens. The invoice for a quote on credit is born with
the **same due date** (approval plus the term's days) and, from then on, that due date
lives on the invoice. A new invoice without a quote proposes the due date from the term you
choose when creating it. In Mexico, an invoice on credit that isn't paid yet is stamped as
**PPD** (see [Invoicing and CFDI](/en/docs/pagos/facturacion)).

## Follow-up at the due date

- **Automatic reminders**, on every plan: your client gets a notice three days before the
  due date and another on the day it's due. See [Reminders](/en/docs/pagos/recordatorios).
- **Notice to your team**: the day after the due date, if it's still unpaid, Cord alerts you
  with the payment overdue notification. See [Notifications](/en/docs/gestion/notificaciones).
- **[AI collections](/en/docs/pagos/cobranza-ia)**, from the Professional plan: if you turn
  it on, the agent includes the overdue account in its daily review and drafts the email with
  the payment link.
- **Late interest**: automatic application is suspended while the legal review by country
  concludes and generates no charges today. See [Receivables and follow-up](/en/docs/pagos/cartera-cobranza).

## Credit limit

Each client can have a **credit limit**. It's a signal, not a block: Cord shows you how much
open balance they carry against that limit on their profile and in Collections, but it doesn't
stop you from quoting them more.

## Limits

- The term counts calendar days, not business days.
- A [recurring retainer](/en/docs/pagos/igualas) only exists with Due on receipt: with a
  credit term, the retainer switch disappears.
- Online payment on the due date depends on your country and on your
  [Cord Payments](/en/docs/pagos/onboarding) or [Mercado Pago](/en/docs/pagos/mercado-pago)
  account being active.

## Troubleshooting

**My client approved and doesn't see the pay button.** That's expected with a credit term:
the button appears on the due date. If you need to collect sooner, issue them an invoice or
switch the quote to Due on receipt before sending it.

**The due date landed on a different day than I expected.** It's counted from the approval
date, not from when you sent it. Check the approval date in the quote's timeline.

**I want a term that isn't on the list, like Net 120.** Today the terms are the seven on this
page. For a different arrangement, use [installments](/en/docs/pagos/cuotas) or set the due
date directly on the invoice.
