The fastest way to quote is not typing faster. It is **stopping the repeated entry of
the same information**. A reliable workflow stores clients, products, taxes, and terms
as reusable data, leaving the seller to decide only what changes in the current deal.

In practice, a fast and controlled quote follows this sequence:

1. select the client;
2. add products or services from a catalog;
3. review price, quantity, discount, and taxes;
4. set validity, payment, delivery, and deposit terms;
5. resolve margin or internal approval requirements;
6. send a traceable link to the client.

## Why copying Word, Excel, or an old quote becomes expensive

Copying a file looks fast because the design is already there. The cost appears later:
the previous buyer's details, outdated prices, broken formulas, inconsistent taxes, or
terms nobody remembered to replace.

Separate three layers instead:

| Layer | Data that belongs there | What to review per deal |
|---|---|---|
| Client | legal name, contact, tax data, currency, credit, price level | contact and exceptional terms |
| Catalog | SKU, description, unit, price, cost, tax | quantity, options, and discount |
| Quote | validity, delivery, deposit, notes, approvals | the exact agreement the client receives |

## Prepare the repeated data first

Configure business identity, country, currency and time zone; brand; numbering; product
catalog; client profiles; default validity and payment terms; and email or WhatsApp
templates. Cord's [complete Settings map](https://docs.cordhq.app/en/docs/cuenta/mapa-de-ajustes)
explains what each control affects.

Start with the client, then add catalog items by name or SKU. Review the description,
quantity, unit, price, discount, taxes, and internal cost or margin when used. A default
helps with entry; it never replaces review.

## Make the commercial conditions explicit

Before sending, state validity, delivery, inclusions and exclusions, deposit, credit or
due date, taxes, currency, and any exchange-rate rule. An approved quote records
commercial acceptance; it **does not become a tax document by itself**.

In Mexico, Cord can issue CFDI 4.0 after the account is configured. In the other offered
markets, it creates a commercial invoice with the available tax identity and numbering.

## Control discounts without stopping every deal

Define what a seller may decide and what requires review. Cord supports thresholds for
discount, amount, and minimum margin. When a document crosses an enabled rule, it enters
the applicable internal approval flow. Approval workflows are available on Scale and
Developer.

## Send a link that lets the client act

A PDF remains useful for downloading and archiving. A public link can also show the
current document, support a conversation, and record approval in the portal. Cord lets
you send by email, copy the link, or share it over WhatsApp.

The list distinguishes draft, sent, viewed, and approved states. A view means that the
link was opened; it does not prove that a person read or understood every section.

## Measure whether the workflow is improving

Track the median time from request to first send, documents returned for data or price
errors, view rate, time from view to decision, average discount, margin conceded, and
recorded loss reasons. Compare equivalent periods and segment by seller, client type,
and deal size.

## Pre-send checklist

- [ ] The client and contact are correct.
- [ ] Number, currency, and validity are consistent.
- [ ] Every line has reviewed quantity, unit, price, and tax.
- [ ] The total matches the selected scope.
- [ ] Deposit, credit, delivery, and exclusions are written down.
- [ ] Margin or internal approval has been resolved.
- [ ] The link works on mobile.
- [ ] The sender and call to action make sense without extra context.
