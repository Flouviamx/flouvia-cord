The world changed, but the B2B sales flow seems to be stuck in 2012.

The account executive gathers requirements, opens Excel or Word, generates a proposal, exports it to PDF, and sends it via email as an attachment. The client receives it, has to download it to their computer, realizes they need to change a quantity, replies to the email requesting the change... and the cycle repeats.

In some teams, a meaningful part of the cycle is spent regenerating files, finding the current version, and confirming changes over email.

## The Friction of the Traditional PDF

Standalone PDFs have clear operating limits:

- **Scattered versions:** a change can create several attachments with similar names.
- **Limited traceability:** email alone does not preserve one current review and approval state.
- **More steps to respond:** requesting changes, printing, or forwarding adds work for the buyer.

The improvement is not eliminating the PDF. It is maintaining one current source and using PDF as a downloadable representation.

## The Transition to Interactive Web Quotes

One alternative is to pair the document with a **secure web link** where the client can review the current state and act.

### Advantages of the interactive model:

1. **Visible state:** Cord records when the link is opened and can notify enabled channels. A view does not prove complete reading or purchase intent.
2. **Modular Options (Integrated Upselling):** Instead of sending three different PDFs ("Basic Option", "Pro Option", "Enterprise Option"), you send a single link where the client can turn modules on or off. The total recalculates right before their eyes.
3. **Approval and payment:** the client can declare their identity and approve. When the account and country are eligible, the balance can offer card payment; automated SPEI applies only to eligible Mexican accounts in MXN. Cord stores technical evidence but does not promise notarial certification or identical legal effect in every jurisdiction.

## The Impact on Sales Cycles

Implementing a "One-Click" approval flow eliminates ambiguity. The client no longer needs to reply to an email saying "Looks good to me, what are the next steps?". The interactive document *is* the next step.

To evaluate the change in your company, measure time to first send, versions per deal, error corrections, time from view to decision, and formatting effort. Compare equivalent periods before attributing improvement to the workflow.

If you are still attaching files in 2026, you are not losing against competitors with a better product; you are losing against competitors who are easier to buy from.
